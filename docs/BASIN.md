# Saint John Basin — Aegis Foundation

Source: Environment Canada, *Monograph on Comprehensive River Basin Planning* (Information Canada, Ottawa, 1975; Cat. En 39-19/1975). Saint John is the worked example (Canada / US / New Brunswick / Quebec / Maine).

Operating card remains [`FIELD.md`](FIELD.md). This file is the why. Do not replace Field language (`pack`, `reserve`, `land`, `never-shed`). If a metaphor does not change a decision, delete it.

Planning must lead to action or it is ill-conceived. These stones govern pack, reserve, land, and continuity — or they are shelf reports.

Work phases map onto the existing loop in [`ABSOLUTE.md`](ABSOLUTE.md) / [`EVOLUTION.md`](EVOLUTION.md): `pack/scrub` → `reuse` → ledger → reserve gate → surplus/ideas → invest → land → audit. Do not invent a parallel loop.

---

## 1. The basin is the unit

**Act.** Water quality, quantity, and land are inseparable. The hydrologic unit is the system. Man-made borders do not define it. Five governments share one river.

**Move.** Plan the whole work-system (code + tokens + Cursor/Hermes/Grok/CLI/menu). Not the chat window.

**Failure.** Edmundston–Madawaska: same mill, two banks, one oxygen collapse.

## 2. Water is not a free good

**Act.** Water was treated as free. Power and waste disposal then dominated every other use.

**Move.** Tokens and attention are water. Price the discharge. Pack/scrub before release. Reserve floor is low-flow protection.

**Failure.** Low-flow summer: the river never recovers from the first dump.

## 3. Comprehensive, not single-purpose

**Act.** Hydro, flood, or irrigation alone proved inadequate. A change in any element is felt in all others. Mactaquac made power; it barred the salmon.

**Move.** Naive path = answer the prompt. Aegis path = pack + reserve + implement-full + land + audit. One win that starves continuity is a dam.

**Failure.** Superb single-purpose report; dead run.

## 4. Oxygen debt

**Act.** Decades of sawdust and mill solids still consume oxygen after effluent drops. Reservoirs look like storage; they cut aeration.

**Move.** Drift, dual sources of truth, unread dumps, and unmeasured “savings” are fiber on the bed. Caches and long context that look like capacity can reduce recovery.

**Failure.** “We stopped dumping” while the bed still bills oxygen.

## 5. Plan the planning, then stay calm

**Act.** Design the process first (objectives, roles, alternatives, schedule, money, openness). Over-design produces continuous panic. Over-refine inventories of the past. Planning looks forward. Deliberate calm process.

**Move.** `preflight` / `pack` before implement. Explore = signatures, not full inventories. Do not design more activity than the reserve can carry.

**Failure.** Panic schedule; another report on the shelf.

## 6. Planning must become action

**Act.** Transition is the most critical stage. History is full of unimplemented superb reports. Involve implementers from design so it is their plan. Interim actions must not pre-empt future options.

**Move.** Implement = full target bodies. `land` closes the loop. Skeleton-only is a shelf plan. Early clever architecture that bars later options is a dam.

**Failure.** Disband after the final report.

## 7. Build bridges; none are permanent

**Act.** No two-way bridge — six lanes both ways. Constant maintenance. Post-planning must be designed in. Keep a liaison core through the lag. A serious planning exercise, once started, never ceases.

**Move.** Continuity Bridge, daemon, receipts, `aegis land`. Never-shed set. Do not kill the planning core when the answer is delivered.

**Failure.** Iron goes cold; funding/context arrives to an empty office.

## 8. Display tradeoffs; do not total incommensurables

**Act.** Multiple objectives (economy, environment, region, welfare). Leave accounts disaggregated. Values are not the expert’s province.

**Move.** Show naive vs Aegis when it matters. `savings_percent` stays null until admitted. No fake single ROI.

**Failure.** One number that hides who pays the oxygen.

## 9. Efficiency, equity, feasibility — existing channels first

**Act.** A theoretically sound scheme is worthless if it cannot be run. Prefer existing agencies over new bureaucracy. Abrupt policy is inequitable. Channel funds to the worst sag first. Joint treatment beats a plant per outfall. Housekeeping often beats new machinery.

**Move.** Reject clever that adds a layer. Smallest patch. Reuse / pack-cache before new modules. ROI-ranked ideas, not uniform spend. Hysteresis, not hard cuts.

**Failure.** Tax incentive for expensive equipment while in-plant housekeeping would cost less.

## 10. The continuing process

**Act.** Planning is iterative. Implementation and new plans run side by side. Success is cost-effective by being cost-avoiding. Begin with sober contemplation of the end-product at work. Feedback may occur from any step.

**Move.** Evolution spine: continuity → justification → same-day yield. Audit after invest. When surplus appears, name the highest-compounding next move.

**Failure.** Treat a shipped report as finished.
